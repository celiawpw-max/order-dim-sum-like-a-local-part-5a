Why this lesson matters

Halfway through yum cha, the little moments count: telling the family the turnip cake is perfect, or flagging a cold dish without fuss. A few short lines cover almost all of it.

🎯 Interaction purpose

Four short, separate exchanges: give varied compliments about the food, describe a problem and ask for a remedy, ask for dirty tableware to be replaced, and request family seating (a BB high chair and one more seat).

✅ By the end you can…

  • Praise a dish with 好正 hou2 zeng3 or 真係好好食 zan1 hai6 hou2 hou2 sik6
  • Say something is a bit off with 有少少凍 jau5 siu2 siu2 dung3
  • Ask for a fix with 可唔可以幫我哋…呀？ ho2 m4 ho2 ji5 bong1 ngo5 dei6 … aa3?
  • Ask for 乾淨 gon1 zeng6 tableware, a BB櫈 bi1 bi1 dang3 and one more seat
Completion goal: give different compliments and handle common table-service requests using clear Hong Kong Cantonese.

🎵 The six tones

The number at the end of each Jyutping syllable tells you the pitch. Tap a tone to hear it.

💡 Tones 136 are flat. Tones 25 are rising. Tone 4 is falling.
🎧 Check your audio settings before recording tap to open
In every step you'll listen to the model, record yourself, then play yours back. You can't move on until each one is done, so set up first:
  1. Allow the microphone. When your browser asks, click Allow. If you clicked Block, use the 🔒 or ⓘ icon by the address bar to turn the microphone back on, then reload.
  2. Pick the right mic. Mac: System Settings → Sound → Input. Windows: Settings → System → Sound → Input. Make sure the level moves when you talk.
  3. Turn the volume up and use headphones if you can, so the playback doesn't echo.
  4. Get a Cantonese voice. The model audio uses your device's Cantonese (Hong Kong) voice. Mac: System Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → System voice → Manage Voices → Chinese (Hong Kong). Windows: Settings → Time & language → Speech → Add voices → Chinese (Traditional, Hong Kong SAR). Chrome or Safari work best.
